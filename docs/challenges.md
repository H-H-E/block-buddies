# Potential Challenges & Risks

## 🔴 Critical (Must Solve Before Launch)

### 1. Child Safety & Liability

**The Problem:**
- Teens interacting with young children online raises safeguarding concerns
- Parents will (rightly) want assurances
- Legal liability for the organization

**Mitigation Strategies:**
- [ ] Parental consent for both mentee AND mentor
- [ ] Video calls only (no private chat/DMs)
- [ ] Parent/guardian must be present or in-room for sessions
- [ ] Background context: teens provide school reference or known adult vouching
- [ ] All sessions recorded (with consent) for safety
- [ ] Clear code of conduct with immediate removal for violations
- [ ] Partner with established youth organizations for credibility (Boys & Girls Club, schools, etc.)

**Open Questions:**
- Do we need formal background checks for teen volunteers? (Probably overkill, but research this)
- What insurance, if any, do we need?
- What's our incident reporting process?

---

### 2. Teen Mentor Quality & Reliability

**The Problem:**
- Teens are busy and may flake on commitments
- Technical skill levels will vary wildly
- Teaching ability ≠ technical ability
- Some teens might sign up just for volunteer hours

**Mitigation Strategies:**
- [ ] Structured onboarding/training for mentors
- [ ] Minimum commitment (e.g., 8-week program, weekly sessions)
- [ ] Mentor-to-mentor support group / Slack channel
- [ ] Pre-built curriculum so mentors aren't starting from scratch
- [ ] "Try a session" before committing
- [ ] Feedback loop from mentees/parents
- [ ] Buddy system: pair new mentors with experienced ones

**Accept that:**
- Some mentors will drop out (build redundancy)
- Quality will vary (focus on minimum viable consistency)

---

### 3. Technical Barriers

**The Problem:**
- Minecraft modding setup is notoriously finicky
- Java development environment can be frustrating
- Different computers, OS versions, Minecraft versions
- Sessions derailed by "it's not working" debugging

**Mitigation Strategies:**
- [ ] Start with simpler mods (resource packs, command blocks, datapacks)
- [ ] Create foolproof setup guides with screenshots
- [ ] Pre-session tech check: make sure everything works BEFORE the teaching session
- [ ] Have parent/tech-savvy adult available for first setup
- [ ] Consider browser-based alternatives (MakeCode for Minecraft Education Edition)
- [ ] Record common troubleshooting solutions

---

## 🟡 Important (Solve Early)

### 4. Matching & Scheduling

**The Problem:**
- Coordinating schedules between busy teens and young kids
- Geographic/timezone differences
- Personality mismatches

**Mitigation Strategies:**
- [ ] Simple matching algorithm: timezone + interests + availability
- [ ] Trial session before full commitment
- [ ] Easy "rematch" process if it's not working
- [ ] Weekly slots that repeat (consistency is key for kids)

---

### 5. Engagement & Retention

**The Problem:**
- Young kids have short attention spans
- Momentum lost between weekly sessions
- Frustration when things don't work

**Mitigation Strategies:**
- [ ] 30-45 minute sessions max
- [ ] Clear "homework" or project to work on between sessions
- [ ] Celebration of milestones (badges, certificates, showcase events)
- [ ] Progress visible to parents
- [ ] Multiple modalities: not just calls, but videos, written guides

---

### 6. Scale & Sustainability

**The Problem:**
- Founders can't do everything forever
- Need consistent pipeline of new mentors as old ones graduate
- Documentation and processes must outlive individuals

**Mitigation Strategies:**
- [ ] Document EVERYTHING from day one
- [ ] Build relationships with schools (renewable mentor supply)
- [ ] Create "mentor alumni" program
- [ ] Eventually: train mentors to train mentors

---

## 🟢 Nice to Solve (Later)

### 7. Geographic/Access Equity

**The Problem:**
- Kids without reliable internet/computers can't participate
- Model assumes access to both Minecraft AND decent hardware

**Future Considerations:**
- Partner with libraries for computer access
- Minecraft Education Edition licensing through schools
- Hardware donation/lending program
- In-person sessions at community centers

---

### 8. Content Moderation

**The Problem:**
- Minecraft servers can have inappropriate content
- User-generated mods may contain issues
- Kids might stumble onto sketchy corners of the internet

**Mitigation Strategies:**
- [ ] Curate approved mods and resources
- [ ] Teach in controlled environments (own server or single-player)
- [ ] Clear guidelines on what sites/content to use

---

### 9. Intellectual Property

**The Problem:**
- Who owns the mods kids create?
- Can we use testimonials/videos for marketing?
- Minecraft trademark usage

**Future Considerations:**
- Simple terms of service
- Media release forms
- Stay on right side of Minecraft EULA
