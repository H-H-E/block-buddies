# Safety & Guidelines

## Core Principles

1. **Transparency**: All interactions should be visible to parents/guardians
2. **Boundaries**: Clear rules about appropriate topics and behavior
3. **Empowerment**: Teach both parties to speak up if something feels wrong
4. **Documentation**: Record everything, report concerns immediately

---

## For Teen Mentors

### The Golden Rules

1. **Never meet in person** without parent/guardian presence and approval
2. **Keep it professional**: You're a tutor, not a friend or big sibling
3. **Video calls only**: No private messaging, DMs, or social media connections
4. **Parents in the loop**: Encourage the mentee's parent to be nearby or watching
5. **Stay on topic**: Minecraft modding, coding, problem-solving. Not personal life.
6. **Document your sessions**: Brief notes after each call

### What to Do If...

| Situation | Action |
|-----------|--------|
| Mentee shares something concerning (abuse, self-harm, etc.) | Don't promise secrecy. Tell them you need to talk to an adult who can help. Report to program coordinator immediately. |
| Mentee asks personal questions | Redirect: "Let's focus on our project! What were we building again?" |
| Mentee wants to add you on social media | "Our program keeps things on these calls, but I'm excited to keep building together here!" |
| You're uncomfortable for any reason | End the session politely. Report to coordinator. No explanation needed. |
| Technical problems eating the whole session | It happens! Document what went wrong, ask for help before next session. |

### Communication Guidelines

**DO:**
- Use encouraging language
- Celebrate small wins
- Ask questions to check understanding
- Be patient with repeated questions
- Use screen sharing to demonstrate

**DON'T:**
- Share your personal contact info
- Ask about family/personal situations
- Make promises you can't keep
- Continue if you're frustrated (take a break)
- Talk down to them

## For Parents/Guardians of Mentees

### Your Responsibilities

1. **Be present**: Stay in the room or within earshot during sessions (especially early on)
2. **Help with setup**: Be available for first-time technical setup
3. **Check in after sessions**: Ask your child how it went, what they learned
4. **Communicate concerns**: Tell us immediately if anything seems off
5. **Respect the mentor's time**: Make sure your child is ready and on time

### What We Promise

- All teen mentors have been vetted and trained
- Sessions follow our structured curriculum
- Video calls are recorded for safety (with your consent)
- You can observe any session
- Clear process for feedback and concerns

### Red Flags to Watch For

- Mentor asking to communicate outside the program
- Requests for personal information
- Sessions going significantly off-topic
- Your child seeming uncomfortable or reluctant
- Mentor making promises (gifts, meetups, etc.)

## For Parents/Guardians of Teen Mentors

### What Your Teen Commits To

- Completing mentor training before starting
- Showing up reliably for scheduled sessions
- Following all safety guidelines
- Communicating with coordinator if issues arise
- Maintaining appropriate boundaries

### What We Provide

- Structured curriculum they can follow
- Training on how to teach and interact safely
- Support from coordinators for any issues
- Documentation of volunteer hours
- Reference letters upon request (after completion)
