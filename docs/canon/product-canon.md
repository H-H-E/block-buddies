# Block Buddies Product & Pedagogy Canon (V3)

This document is the authoritative source of truth for what Block Buddies is, who it serves, and how it teaches. When any other file conflicts with this canon, this canon wins. Legacy documents that disagree are historical or advanced-pathway material, not current policy.

## Intended Participants

- **Learners:** younger children, beginning around age 7 (pilot beginner band 6-8; older children may join through appropriate entry points).
- **Mentors:** teens aged 14-18.
- **Primary format:** one mentor with one child. Carefully controlled small-group and shared-world variants are allowed where they serve the relationship.

## Program Promise

A relatable older Minecraft player helps your child feel comfortable in Minecraft, make things they are proud of, and gradually learn to follow tutorials and finish projects more independently - through a warm, safe, ongoing relationship.

## Mentor and Learner Roles

**The child is always the primary maker and decision-maker.**

The mentor may: model, narrate, ask questions, gather repetitive materials, demonstrate one step, help recover broken states, build a supporting section, and celebrate/document progress. The mentor must not take over the child's account, complete the interesting parts, or turn sessions into a performance.

Guardians own account setup, platform readiness, consent, and communication. Coordinators own matching, safeguarding escalation, and adult-owned world/server administration.

## Developmental Assumptions

For the age-7 beginner band:

- Low reading and typing demands; icons, screenshots, and mentor narration over text.
- One action per visible instruction; spoken, pointed, demonstrated, or screenshot evidence.
- Tutorial segments of roughly 2-5 minutes before the child acts.
- Sessions of approximately 30-45 minutes with flexible stopping points.
- Concrete bounded choices rather than broad open-ended prompts when needed.
- Clear controller, touch, keyboard/mouse distinctions.
- No session consumed by account, launcher, network, or installation troubleshooting; immediate fallback activities when technology fails.
- Clarity, never baby talk.

## Minecraft-First Priorities

The central value is the relationship with a relatable older player. Sessions teach Minecraft through mentorship. Computer science, mathematics, design, systems thinking, digital literacy, and executive-function skills may appear naturally during play; they are optional connections, never the governing purpose or a visible mandatory child assessment.

Priority hierarchy when trade-offs arise:

1. Child safety and trust
2. Quality of the mentor-child relationship
3. Enjoyment, agency, and continued interest
4. Minecraft competence and project completion
5. Tutorial-following and independent-learning skills
6. Mentor communication and leadership growth
7. Optional academic or technical connections

A lesson that teaches an elegant concept but weakens the Minecraft experience or the relationship is badly designed.

## Session Structure

Every beginner session follows this rhythm (adapted freely within the time budget):

1. **Reconnect** - talk about what the child played, made, watched, or wants to try.
2. **Choose today's shared goal** - offer two or three concrete choices when the child has none.
3. **Quick win** - something visible or satisfying within ~5-10 minutes.
4. **Watch or model** - a very short approved video segment or one small mentor demonstration.
5. **Predict and try** - the child predicts, attempts, and controls the game.
6. **Build or play together** - toward a shared artifact, child as maker.
7. **Remix** - the child changes at least one meaningful feature.
8. **Show and celebrate** - capture and let the child show or describe it.
9. **Choose what happens next** - record the next desired project or continuation.

Child-facing language uses Make / Show / Tell / Try / Fix / Choose. Do/Explain/Debug survives only as an internal mentor observation tool, never a visible mandatory child assessment. Seeded faults are not required in every module; debugging appears only when it serves the activity.

Preserved strong ideas from V2: early visible success, progressive hints, bounded frustration, fallback mini-wins, prepared mentor scripts, safe reset paths, accessibility alternatives, child choice, concrete artifacts, parent summaries, flexible pacing, mentor preparation, continuation notes.

## Content Selection Principles

- Bedrock-first pilot: tablets, consoles, and family devices are the norm.
- Every module declares supported editions, platforms, versions, input methods, multiplayer requirements, and known incompatibilities.
- Never mix Java and Bedrock instructions in one module.
- Java-only material belongs in an optional older/advanced pathway; no pretended parity.
- Videos are structured, reviewed resources with exact segments, pause points, and no-video fallbacks - never loose links, recommendations browsing, comments, or arbitrary external links during sessions.

## Optional Concept Connections

Academic/technical connections are recorded as optional tags in module metadata. Mentors may surface them conversationally when the child is curious. They must not become lectures, gates, or requirements.

## Safety Requirements

- Guardian-visible communication only; no mentor-child direct messages.
- No exchange of personal social accounts; approved video-call channels only.
- Private or allowlisted worlds; adult-owned server/Realm administration; least-privilege mentor permissions.
- No public-server discovery, port forwarding, router changes, IP publication, VPN tunnelling, unknown downloads, learner admin credentials, or account sharing.
- Clear pause, restore, report, and escalate procedures; guardian help for account/platform setup.
- Data minimization; explicit recording and media-consent rules; full-session recording is NOT the default evidence mechanism.
- Separate ordinary Minecraft conflict from safeguarding incidents.

See docs/safety-guidelines.md for the operational policy layer.

## Evidence & Assessment Philosophy

Evidence exists to celebrate progress and plan continuity, not to grade the child. Spoken descriptions, pointing, demonstration, screenshots, and artifacts all count. Evidence is captured briefly by the mentor and shared with guardians as plain-language summaries. Technically valid content is distinguished from human-playtested content in every review status.

## Progression Model

Progression is a module library plus interest-led pathways, not a fixed numbered course. After three common foundation modules, children choose interest-led projects (building, survival/animals, redstone/storytelling, and more). Tracks such as Visualist/Mechanist/Operator survive only as optional interest tags or advanced pathways - never permanent child identities. Matching uses hard constraints first (edition, platform, availability, safety) and human-reviewed soft matching second.

## Public-Language Rules

- Say "Minecraft mentorship", not "coding class disguised as Minecraft".
- Ages: learners "starting around age 7"; mentors "14-18".
- No fake testimonials, unsupported metrics, or implied persistence/integration that does not exist.
- Family-friendly framing; internal pedagogical shorthand stays internal.

## Non-Goals

- Not a computer-science course, systems-administration course, or bootcamp.
- Not a public multiplayer service; no unmanaged chat or discovery.
- Not a replacement for guardian involvement in account/platform setup.
- No full-session recording as the default evidence mechanism.
- Do not build heavy backend persistence until the pilot demands it; label deferred work honestly.
